Vt-adjust boron implant

Boron ions are shot into the p-type substrate (N_sub = 1e16 cm⁻³). Energy sets how deep they stop, dose sets how many. The result is a Gaussian, reflected at the surface so the whole dose stays in silicon.

Activation anneal

The wafer sits in a furnace and the boron diffuses. Diffusivity is Arrhenius, so it is very steep in temperature: about 25–30 % faster per +10 °C. The furnace runs at setpoint plus the hidden offset plus a ±1 °C run-to-run wobble.

30oxidation

Dry gate oxidation

Grows the gate oxide by the Deal-Grove model on (100) silicon, starting from 1.5 nm of native oxide. This step does three things at once: it sets the oxide thickness, it adds more thermal budget (the boron keeps diffusing), and it eats 0.44 nm of silicon per nm grown, which removes the top of the boron profile.

40profile

Final channel doping profile

A Gaussian stays Gaussian under constant diffusion, so the two heat steps simply widen it. The profile is then measured from the new silicon surface, below the consumed layer. This N(x) is what the transistor sees.

σ² = ΔRp² + 2·(Dt_anneal + Dt_ox) N(x) on a 0 – 1 µm grid (801 points), x measured below the Si/SiO₂ interface

The coupling lives here: the oxidation knobs change both t_ox (step 30) and σ (this step), so they move Vt in two directions at once.

60depletion

Depletion region at threshold

Solves Poisson's equation numerically on the real, non-uniform profile: how deep does the depletion region reach before the surface potential hits 2φ_F? Only the boron inside that depth raises Vt. This is the integral that shortcuts like ΔVt = qQ/C_ox skip.

φ_F = (kT/q)·ln(N_sub / n_i) = 0.357 V find W_d such that (q/ε_si) · ∫₀^W_d x·N(x) dx = 2φ_F Q_dep = q · ∫₀^W_d N(x) dx

Threshold voltage, with short-channel roll-off

Long-channel Vt from flat-band voltage, surface potential and depletion charge. Short gates then lose Vt to roll-off and drain-induced barrier lowering, which grows exponentially as L approaches a few characteristic lengths.

Off current and on current

Off current falls one decade per subthreshold swing S of extra Vt. On current uses a velocity-saturation model with mobility that degrades in high vertical field. Short L and thin oxide raise Ion; low Vt raises both Ion and Ioff.

S = ln10 · (kT/q) · (1 + C_dep/C_ox), C_dep = ε_si / W_d Ioff = 1e-7 A · (W/L) · 10^(−Vt / S) μ_eff = 450 / (1 + 0.12 · (10 nm / t_ox) · (Vdd − Vt)) cm²/Vs E_c = 2·v_sat / μ_eff, v_sat = 8e6 cm/s Ion = W · C_ox · v_sat · (Vdd − Vt)² / ((Vdd − Vt) + E_c · L)

Layer 3 · Wafer and variation

one device → 68 dies → many wafers
90wafer

Steps 10–80 run for every die on every wafer

A 100 mm wafer with 9 mm dies and 3 mm edge exclusion holds 68 dies. Each run_wafer call draws its own run-to-run noise, then each die draws its own die-to-die noise, so no two dies are identical. This is why a recipe has to sit in the middle of the spec window, not on its edge.

Step 100 on chosen dies. This is the only window into the hidden state.

submit_recipe(recipe)ends the episode

Step 110. One shot. Nothing carries over except the recipe.

What a strong run does. In level 3 it runs a cold-anneal and a hot-anneal test wafer, measures oxide, sheet resistance and Vt, and works back to the furnace offsets and dose factor. Then it optimises the documented model with those offsets, preferring recipes that tolerate the variation in step 90. It verifies once and submits, spending about 4 budget units.

Where a weak run goes wrong. It tunes one knob at a time while the oxidation knobs move Vt two ways (steps 30 and 40). It uses ΔVt = qQ/C_ox and misses step 60 by a factor of two. It trusts setpoints and lands 15 °C off the window. It reads one die and takes noise for signal.
